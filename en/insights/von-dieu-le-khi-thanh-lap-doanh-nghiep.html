Charter Capital: How Much Should a New Company Register?

Updated 08/19/20266 min readPrepared by BizLegal

How to set charter capital based on operating needs, funding commitments and sector-specific requirements.

In this article

01Minimum capital is not universal02Use operational assumptions03Track contribution and later changes

Charter capital is a legal and commercial commitment. A figure that is unrealistically high or too low can both create avoidable problems.

Minimum capital is not universal

Many ordinary business lines do not prescribe a fixed minimum. Certain regulated sectors, however, may require legal capital, deposits or proof of financial capacity.

Use operational assumptions

Estimate initial expenses, working capital, contractual commitments and the owners’ actual ability to contribute. Consider how the declared amount will appear to banks, partners and licensing authorities.

Track contribution and later changes

Owners should contribute capital within the applicable statutory period and keep evidence of payment. If plans materially change, review whether a capital adjustment and related corporate actions are needed.

Key point

Register an amount that is supportable, traceable and appropriate for the planned activity.
